Publications 学术出版物

Beyond and slightly aside of the Fermat desert

Bartolomé, Boris and Chang Liu and Preda Mihăilescu*

Under review, 2025 审稿中, 2025

The abstract describes an approach to Fermat's Conjecture utilizing methods restricted to the class field theory of the p-cyclotomic field. These methods were known over a century ago, and the proof presented is described as a "remote echo" of results from the early 20th century. The paper discusses how previous proofs relied on independent and powerful general theorems with broad mathematical consequences, whereas this work employs more specialized methods. It also references the "Fermat desert" as a metaphor for the long, barren period of research on the conjecture.

The diophantine equation $(2^{k}-1)(b^{k}-1)=y^{q}$

Chang Liu and Bo He*

Preprint, under review (arXiv:2512.00548), 2025 预印本, 审稿中 (arXiv:2512.00548), 2025

This paper discusses the exponential Diophantine equation $(2^k-1)(b^k-1)=y^q$ where $k \ge 2$, $b$ is an odd integer, and $q$ is an odd prime exponent. The authors aim to derive effective upper bounds for $q$ in terms of $b$. Specifically, they demonstrate that $q \le \log_2(b+1)$ generally holds, with the exception of a finite, explicitly determined set of exceptional pairs $(b,q)$ when $3 \le b < 10^6$. As an application, they prove that the related equation $(2^k-1)(b^k-1)=x^n$ has no positive integer solution $(k,x,n)$ for several specific odd values of $b$, including $b \in \{5, 7, 11, 13, 21, 23, 27, 29\}$.

The diophantine equation $(2^k-1)(3^k-1)=x^n$

He, Bo and Chang Liu*

Periodica Mathematica Hungarica, 2025

The authors investigated the Diophantine equation $(2^k-1)(3^k-1)=x^n$ and proved that it has no solutions in positive integers $k, x$, and $n$ where $n > 2$.

An alternative proof of the Catalan conjecture

Chang Liu and Preda Mihăilescu*

Bulletin of the Transilvania University of Braşov, 2025

This paper offers a simplified approach to Mihăilescu's original proof of the Catalan conjecture. The original proof, published in 2004, utilized real binomial power series expansions and the annihilation of real class groups, relying on a theorem by Thaine. The alternative proof by Liu and Mihăilescu employs Stickelberger annihilation of the minus part of the class group, thereby eliminating the need for Thaine's Theorem. This approach is founded on demonstrating the existence of a character that connects Galois exponents of roots of unity.
